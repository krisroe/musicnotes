First Council of Nicaea (325)

convened by Emperor Constantine, who had legalized Christianity in the Roman Empire via the Edict of Milan (313). Despite not being a bishop, he presided and enforced the council's outcomes.

0. Short version: Jesus is fully divine.
1. Arianism rejected, Arius condemned as a heretic. Jesus is fully divine. Not a created being, but "begotten, not made" and "of the same substance" (homoousios) as the Father. 
2. Nicene Creed (first universal Christian creed)
2a. Belief in one God, the Father Almighty
2b. Belief in one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God
2c. Jesus is “God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God”
2d. Jesus was “begotten, not made”
2e. Jesus was “of one substance with the Father” (homoousios)
2f. Jesus became human, was crucified, rose again, and ascended into heaven
3. Date of Easter standardized (separating from Jewish Passover calendar), aligning to the Sunday after the first full moon following the vernal equinox
4. Church Discipline and Structure (20 canons)

First Council of Constantinople (381)

0. Short version: The Holy Spirit is fully divine, part of the Trinity.
1. Condemned
1a. Pneumatomachians ("Spirit-fighters"). Holy Spirit was a created being or subordinate to the Father and the Son.
1b. Apollinarianism – claimed Jesus had a human body and soul, but no human mind (replaced by divine Logos, like God “wearing a human suit”).
1c. Macedonianism – denied the divinity of the Holy Spirit.
1d. Sabellianism (implicitly) – taught that the Father, Son, and Spirit are just modes of one God, not distinct persons.
2. Bishop of Constantinople made second in rank after the Bishop of Rome, “because Constantinople is New Rome”. Annoyed Patriarch of Alexandria.

Council of Ephesus (431)
0. Short version: Mary is Mother of God (she gave birth to the person of Jesus who is both God and man) (Jesus is one person).
1. Nestorianism, rejected as heretic. He preferred Mother of Christ instead.

Council of Chalcedon (451)

1. Definition of Chalcedon... "Acknowledged in two natures, without confusion, without change, without divison, without separation."
1a. Two natures: Jesus is fully divine and fully human
1b. One person (hypostasis): These two natures exist in one unified person
1c. Without confusion/change. His natures aren't blended into a third hybrid nature.
1d. Without division/separation. His divine and human natures aren't split into two persons.
2. Condemned Monophysitism (Jesus only has one nature which is the divine absorbing human)

Second Council of Constantinople (553)

0. The unity of the person must be strongly emphasized.
1. Condemned the "Three Chapters" of Nestorian-leaning interpretations of Chalcedon. They were accepted at Chalcedond so condemning them looked as though Chalcedon was being revised.
1a. Theodore of Mopsuestia
1b. Theodoret of Cyrrhus
1c. Ibas of Edessa
2. Reaffirmed Nicene Creed, Definition of Chalcedon, but interpreted through St. Cyril of Alexandria (champion of the "one incarnate nature" formula)
However, this poses a problem since if Jesus lacks a human will, then he's not fully human.

Third Council of Constantinople (680–681)
0. Jesus has two wills (divine and human) perfectly united in one person.
1. Condemned Monothelitism (Jesus has two natures but only one will—this view is called Monothelitism (mono = one, thelema = will)). 

Second Council of Nicaea (787)
0. Affirmed the veneration (not worship) of icons.
1. Distinguished between Latria (worship) -> due to God alone and Proskynesis (veneration/honor) to icons/saints/relics. Venerating an icon passes to the prototype.
2. Declared iconoclasm heretical
3. Cited incarnation as theological basis. Because God became visible in Jesus Christ, it is now theologically appropriate to depict him visually.
4. Restored the use of icons in churches and homes

Iconoclasm wasn’t totally over. After Irene’s reign, a second wave of iconoclasm hit in the 800s under Emperor Leo V. It wasn’t until 843 AD, during the Triumph of Orthodoxy, that icon veneration was finally and permanently restored in the Byzantine Church.

Fourth Council of Constantinople
(869–870). Recognized by Roman Catholic Church. Deposed Photius (Patriarch of Constantinople)
(879-880). Recognized by Eastern Orthodox Church. Restored Photius.

First Lateran Council (1123)
1. Ratified the Concordat of Worms
1a. Confirmed that lay investiture (kings appointing bishops) was officially forbidden.
1b. Reinforced that only the Church can grant spiritual authority (symbolized by the ring and staff).
1c. Secular rulers could still have some involvement, but only in civil matters like land and protection.
2. Clerical Reforms
2a. Reaffirmed clerical celibacy—no priests could marry.
2b. Condemned simony—the buying and selling of Church offices or sacraments.
2c. Declared that only validly elected popes and bishops had authority—trying to stop secular influence and anti-popes.
3. Protection of Church Property and Persons. Issued canons calling for the protection of churches and monasteries, clergy, pilgrims, church lands and possessions.

Arianism.: Arius, a priest from Alexandria, taught that Jesus Christ was not co-eternal with God the Father, meaning there was a time when the Son did not exist. Quote: "There was a time when the Son was not." Survived to ~700 AD.
Jehovah's Witnesses: Jesus is a created being (first and greatest creation of God/Jehovah). Jesus is not Almighty God. Deny trinity.
Unitarians: Jesus was a human being, perhaps divinely inspired

Assyrian Church of the East (offshoot of Nestorian). Christ is one person with two natures: divine and human. These are distinct but united in one person, the Son of God. May prefer "Mother of Christ", not because they deny Jesus' divinity, but to emphasize both natures. Jesus is not two persons; he is one person with two complete, united natures. This has been reconciled with orthodox Christianity as a vocabulary difference.

Oriental Orthodox Churches (offshoot of Monophysitism). Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean, and Malankara Indian churches. Call themselves Miaphysite. (Jesus' divine and human natures are united in one composite nature, without loss or confusion)

Human Body: self-explanatory
Human Soul: Gives life, provides consciousness, emotions, desires, impulses
Human Mind (rational faculty): thinks, understands, makes choices, knows right/wrong, connects with God through reason/contemplation

Filioque Clause = "and the Son"
We believe in the Holy Spirit who proceeds from the Father ([Filioque clause] and the Son)
Eastern Orthodox position: The Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone. Filioque implies two sources in the Trinity, which distorts the balance. Furious that the West added to the Nicene Creed unilaterally, without an ecumenical council (breach of canon law and tradition)
Western Catholic position: The Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. Filioque expresses the oneness of the Father and Son and their shared divinity.
