Silent Night (Christian Christmas Hymn) (English translation)
Words by Joseph Mohr
Music by Franz Xaver Gruber (1818)

Silent night. Holy night.
All is calm. All is bright.
Round yon virgin, Mother and child.
Holy infant, so tender and mild.
Sleep in heavenly peace.
Sleep in heavenly peace.

Silent night. Holy night.
Shepherd's quake at the sight.
Glories stream from heaven afar.
Heavenly hosts sing "Alleluia!"
Christ the Savior is born.
Christ the Savior is born.

Silent night. Holy night.
Son of God, love's pure light.
Radiant beams from thy holy face,
With the dawn of redeeming grace.
Jesus, Lord, at thy birth.
Jesus, Lord, at thy birth.

Meaning: This is a Christian Christmas song referencing the birth of Jesus. This is captured by an image of the nativity, with the baby Jesus in the central position,
surrounded by the mother (immaculately conceived) and father. Farm animals sharing the space with the family. Shepherds and wise men have been drawn to
the space. It is arguably the holiest scene in Christianity, the birth of someone both divine and human, bathed in angelic light, inevitably destined to be the Savior
Lord redeeming the human sin.

Part of the holyness comes from the silence of the scene. A newborn baby and animals, be they cows, sheep, or camels, can be very noisy, but not in this context.
But the song itself generates noise, and refers to heavenly hosts singing "Alleluia". So maybe it's not a silent night, more of a scene where the noise of
the human world gives way to the noise of the divine.
