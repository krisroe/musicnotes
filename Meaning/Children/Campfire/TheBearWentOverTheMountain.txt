The Bear Went Over the Mountain (Campfire Song)

The bear went over the mountain.
The bear went over the mountain.
The bear went over the mountain
To see what he could see.

The other side of the mountain,
The other side of the mountain,
The other side of the mountain,
Was all that he could see.

Meaning: This song describes the exploits of a curious male bear who climbs a mountain. This seems like it would be a difficult exploit, since climbing mountains is difficult for humans, and bears lack opposable thumbs. Nevertheless this particular bear wants to see from the mountain top, and is resourceful and determined enough to succeed.

The stereotypical view from a mountaintop is you can see distant places, even beyond the mountain itself, in a spectacular view. But it seems for this bear, the only vista is the other side of the mountain. Why is the bear's vision so limited? Perhaps the weather is poor. Perhaps the bear is not interested in the world beyond the mountain. Perhaps bears do not understand the world enough to truly take in what's in visible range.

Or (speculating), perhaps the limitation is not on the bear, the bear sees the other side of the mountain is what is truly important. The bear has accomplished a difficult impressive feat of conquering the mountain. And now the bear can choose to go back the way he came, or can go down the other side, possibly in any direction. Either way, it's a way down, and going downhill is stereotypically an easier road than uphill. But this is still a mountain we're talking about, so going downhill too fast entails difficulty and risk as well. Maybe the bear is excited about going back downhill, or maybe the really difficult part of the journey has just begun.
