The song refers to the split halves of the human brain/mind, and the emotions (that can be challenging to handle, even for the most resilient of us) that lie within. Emotions are a fundamental thing that makes humans human, and can be required for effective human functioning. But humans don't feel emotions at the same potency at all times and different people have different capacity for emotions. If someone can't feel your emotions well, or even at all (because of the stresses you face), using the logical part of your mind to fill gaps is a useful and sometimes necessary skill.

The song mentions several relevant emotions (and one emotion-adjacent concept)
Refrain #1. Pain (not an emotion but can be felt just as strongly, both for physical and psychological suffering).
Refrain #2. Love.
Refrain #3. Fear.
Refrain #4. Hatred. There's a line where anger (#5b) crosses a threshold of intensity and becomes separate. Anger is hard enough to cope with, but dealing with hatried in ourselves or others is part of being human all the same.
Refrain #5a. Sadness.
Refrain #5b. Anger ("mad" in the line following "feel the sad")

An important line in the song is "Communication, both sides" which happens twice in the final section and is not sung lyrically. Communication is important in resolving conflicts between people (or any kind of relationship), and it's also important in resolving conflicts within oneself. The human brain/mind is stereotypically split between logical and emotional sides. The parts don't always agree, and communication (usually subconscious) is helpful for resolving that two.

The final line of the song is simply "Communication," after which the song cuts out. The intent is the listener fill in the end of the song with an implied "both sides.". The song, despite its suggestion that communication is important, cuts off its own communication at the end. But that's reflective of reality, too, communication isn't always possible for multiple reasons. Sometimes creative measures are needed to keep communication lines open, so even when it's not possible to talk things out or write things out or nonverbally work things out, to ensure the functional work of communication is performed.
