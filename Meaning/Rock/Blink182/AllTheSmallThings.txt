Meaning: "All the Small Things" refers to many small seemingly insignificant details that collectively substantively improve life. The song refers to details
both inside and outside the context of a relationship. Small favors show one truly cares about others. The song primarily describes the impact of the small
things on the songwriter, but can be generalized to a wider audience of the listener, although not without losing some of the personal-ness.

The first verse mentions a very general favor of giving someone a ride, and also considers going a band's show as a small show of appreciation for the band.
But the primary interpretation is more personal for the songwriter. A well-known band member is not going to get a ride with just anybody and only a close personal 
friend can sympathize/commiserate with the true stresses of a famous band. But if the band member's life is sufficiently public, perhaps fans can commiserate too.

The second verse sets up a relationship situation where someone gets home late from unpleasant work demands and their partner left roses on the stairs (inspired by an 
actual incident where one of the songwriter's girlfriend did exactly that, which is a role reversal of the tradition of the male partner getting flowers for the female 
partner). Surprises can be away of showing affection toward another.

"Carry me home" can refer more generally to the idea that others metaphorically carry us through life's stresses. A person can also be metaphorically described as 
a "little windmill", providing power to another allowing them to power through life's difficulties. Windmills also continually spin in difficult weather, so the windmill
metaphor also works for some providing continual support to another.

Although many parts of the song have a nonsexualized interpretation, the primary interpretation is personal and sexual in the context of a relationship.
"Say it ain't so" and "I will not go" are both phrases that can be uttered in the context of conflict in a romantic relationship. Sex can be used to maintain
trust and love even in the presence of conflict. The main lyrics and third verse have strong signs of sexual interpretation. "Turn the lights off" is a common environment 
for sexual activity. "Carry me home" easily evokes an image of a male partner literally carrying a female partner into the bedroom. And all three verses use the chorus.

The third verse is particularly sexualized: "Keep your head still" is a command that could occur as part of a sex act (e.g. oral sex). "I'll be your thrill" could refer 
to one partner's pleasuring one another. "The night will go on, my little windmill" could refer to a (possibly abnormally long) time a sex act could be performed.

So there are sexualized and nonsexualized interpretations, and personal songwriter and generic interpretations. If you're not willing or capable of going into the sexualized 
part (or the songwriter personal part) you can still interpret the song to have meaning, but your interpretation will be missing something over the original form.
